Gentle Reflection Journal

This journal is designed to gently guide you toward a deeper connection with your body and your emotions. These prompts prioritize safety and self-compassion, helping you build a foundation for healing.

💡 Gentle Reminder: There are no right or wrong answers. You can work at your own pace and use the export or share options to save your responses. This is your space for curious, compassionate self-reflection.

1. Understanding Your Protectors 🛡️

Consider a part of you that is highly self-critical or anxious. Instead of pushing it away, get curious. What is this part afraid would happen if it stopped doing its job? What is its positive intention in trying to protect you?

2. Finding Your Window of Tolerance 🏞️

Describe a time today or this week when you felt calm, present, and engaged—not overwhelmed, and not shut down. What did that state of "just right" feel like in your body and mind?

3. Identifying a "Felt Sense" of Safety 🏡

What does true "emotional safety" feel like in your body? Describe the physical sensations and thoughts that accompany this feeling of security.

4. Listening to Your Body's Signals 🧘

Pause and bring your attention inward. Without needing a reason, ask your body what it needs right now. Is it asking for a stretch, a glass of water, a deep breath, or a moment of stillness?

5. Attuning to Your Inner Child ❤️

Without focusing on a past event, what does your "inner child" need from you today to feel safe, seen, and loved?

6. Understanding Coping Mechanisms ☕

Describe a "surrogate comfort" you use when feeling stressed (e.g., scrolling, shopping). What deeper emotional need—such as nurturing, safety, or comfort—might this behavior be trying to meet?

7. Examining the Purpose of a Belief ⚙️

Choose a belief that causes you pain. What purpose might this belief have served in the past? How might it have helped you make sense of the world or survive a difficult situation at one time?

8. Meeting Your Needs in the Moment 🎁

What is your primary unmet emotional need today: nurturing, safety, or inner guidance? How can you offer that to yourself using your five senses?

9. The Experience of Receiving Care 🤲

Reflect on a recent moment when someone offered you genuine kindness, a compliment, or support. What did it feel like in your body to receive it?

10. Cultivating Self-Compassion 🙏

Write a compassionate message to yourself, acknowledging your courage for engaging in healing work and affirming that your pace is the right pace.

DBT-Aligned Skill Prompts

These prompts are designed to help you actively apply concrete skills from Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT) to build resilience and ground yourself in the present moment.

1. The Proactive Application of TIPP

Imagine an intense emotional wave rising — maybe one you've felt before. What physiological signals might you notice in your body? The TIPP skill (Temperature, Intense exercise, Paced breathing, Paired muscle relaxation) offers some doorways. Which ones feel like they could work for you, and how would you move through them?

2. Mindful Self-Care with the PLEASE Skill

Reflect on your past 24 hours with gentleness. The DBT "PLEASE" skill invites us to notice how the body was tended — Physical health, EATing, Avoiding mood-altering substances, SLEEP, Exercise. Where was your body well cared for? Where was it asked to carry more than it had? There's no grade here — just curiosity.

3. Assertive Communication with the FAST Skill

Think of a relationship where something needs to be said or a boundary needs to be held. The DBT "FAST" skill offers a frame: Fair, no unnecessary Apologies, Stick to your values, Truthful. You might sketch what you'd want to say — rehearsing it on paper can make the real conversation feel a little more possible.

4. Finding Freedom in Radical Acceptance

Describe a situation in your life that you find yourself repeatedly fighting against or trying to change, even though you know you have no control over it. Experiment with radical acceptance of this reality. How can you distinguish between accepting the reality of the situation and condoning it? Write about the suffering you might be able to let go of if you choose to stop fighting this unchangeable reality.

5. Non-Judgmental Observation of Thoughts

For the next five minutes, simply observe the thoughts that pass through your mind. As a thought arises, you can label it—for example, calling it 'a planning thought' or 'a memory thought.' Don't follow the thought or get caught up in it; just observe and label. What do you notice about the thoughts themselves? Did you feel any physical sensations as you observed them?

This journal is your personal tool for compassionate self-discovery. Use it at your own pace and bring your reflections to our next session.

Companion Resources

These are some of the voices that have been companions to this work. None of them is homework. Take what serves. Leave what doesn't.

Books

  • DBT Skills Training Handouts and Worksheets by Marsha Linehan — the foundational DBT workbook, adapted for self-guided use. Good if you want to work one skill at a time at your own pace.
  • The Dialectical Behavior Therapy Skills Workbook by Matthew McKay, Jeffrey Wood, and Jeffrey Brantley — a softer entry point than Linehan. Plain language, short chapters, easy to dip into and set down.
  • Self-Compassion: The Proven Power of Being Kind to Yourself by Kristin Neff — pairs naturally with the radical acceptance work. Helpful when the inner critic gets loud around the skills themselves.

Online practices

  • Therapist Aid — free DBT and CBT worksheets when a particular skill wants more structure than this journal offers.
  • Self-Compassion — Dr. Neff's site, with free guided practices including the Self-Compassion Break.
